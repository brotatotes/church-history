About and method

This timeline shows 198 Christian churches, movements and families and 51 events, from the first century to the present. Every date, relationship and description shown on a card comes from a cited source, and every citation links to the exact excerpt that supports it. The site quotes 1059 excerpts from 9 works.

Sources

Only an approved list of works is used. Older reference works in the public domain, such as Schaff's History of the Christian Church, the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia and the 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica, are quoted at greater length. Modern works in copyright, such as the Pew Research Center reports and the Association of Religion Data Archives, are quoted only in a sentence or two. A church's own published history is used only to describe how the church describes itself, never as proof of a date. Wikipedia is not used. The full list is on the bibliography page.

How each entry was researched

Each entry was researched from scratch. A fact appears only when an approved source states it, and the supporting words are stored with the fact. Every excerpt was then fetched again from the original and checked word for word, and each value was checked against what its excerpt actually says. Wording was kept plain and neutral. Census figures were compared with images of the printed pages.

When sources disagree, the card marks the date as disputed and shows each view with its own citation. When no approved source could be found for a date or relationship, the field is left out rather than guessed.

Entries still being researched

11 entries are marked Still being researched. They appear so the timeline is complete, but they lack a sourced start date or relationship. They will be filled in when an approved source is found.

Reading the chart

  • Each line runs from a group's sourced start date to its end date or to the present. Lines do not show size.
  • Where a source gives only a century or a decade, the line is placed at an approximate position. The card always shows the source's own wording.
  • The time scale is compressed before 1500 so that later centuries have room.
  • Movements that crossed church boundaries, such as Pietism or the Holiness movement, are drawn as shaded bands across the churches they influenced. They can be switched on and off under Filters.
  • Groups whose teaching departs from the Nicene and Chalcedonian creeds are shown in a separate, muted lane labelled “Outside the historic creeds (Nicene and Chalcedonian)”. This is a description of doctrinal position, not a judgement.
  • Lane headings and colours are for navigation only.
